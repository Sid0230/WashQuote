Pressure Washing Pricing Guide

There is no single universal pressure washing price. A useful starting point is to calculate what the job actually costs your business and then set a margin that supports the business.

1. Calculate direct labor

Estimate hours and workers. Include the labor cost your business actually bears.

2. Add job-specific costs

Include chemicals, materials, fuel or travel and other job costs.

3. Allocate overhead

Equipment, insurance, software, administration and other operating expenses still need to be covered.

4. Set a target margin

Use the margin that fits your business model, then compare the resulting quote with local market conditions and the specific job.

5. Use a minimum charge when appropriate

Small jobs can consume travel and setup time disproportionately, so a minimum charge can be part of your pricing system.
